## 3. DOES THIS HAVE ANYTHING TO DO WITH DATA CENTERS?

**Honest answer: the current advisory is NOT caused by data centers — and no official source claims it is.**

- The documented cause is a physical **water main break** (Oct) and **internal power failures** (May, July) at aging City of Atlanta water facilities.
- The water system is **City of Atlanta DWM** infrastructure serving south Fulton wholesale — not a data-center facility, not a DC contractor.
- A resident's Instagram comment ("those damn data centers already giving us issues") is **speculation, not evidence.** We do not repeat it as fact.

**What IS legitimate to argue (structural, not causal):**

1. **The water system is already fragile.** Three boil advisories in five months — two from power loss at pump stations — is not a healthy system. This is documented, on-the-record fragility.
2. **Data centers concentrate the exact stressors that break water systems:**
   - **Power draw** — Georgia Power's 10,000 MW buildout is ~80% for data centers. Grid stress is a known risk to pump-station reliability (the May and July events were power failures, even if "internal").
   - **Water draw** — hyperscale DCs use millions of gallons daily for evaporative cooling. Adding that load to an already-fragile regional water system is a real engineering risk, not fear-mongering.
3. **The city's own "Understanding Data Centers" page is now impossible to take at face value.** It claims *"No environmental risks have been found that would negatively impact the community"* and *"There will be no increase in electric or water bills for Union City residents."* A community under its third boil advisory of the year, sitting next to a $15B grid buildout, is entitled to ask: **risks "not found" by whom, and under what standard?** "No risk found" is not "no risk."

**The framing we run with (accurate + persuasive):**
> The boil advisories are not the data centers' fault — *yet.* They are the canary. They prove the water/power system Union City depends on is already near its edge. We should not stack 20+ industrial-scale data centers' power and water demand on top of a system that just failed three times in five months.

## 4. FACT-CHECK — Union City's "Understanding Data Centers" page

City's own page (unioncityga.gov/Explore-UC/Understanding-Data-Centers) makes three claims worth pushing back on, now with receipts:

| City claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| "No environmental risks have been found" | "Not found" ≠ "none." Three boil advisories in 5 months is live evidence of water/power fragility the page never addresses. |
| "There will be no increase in electric or water bills for residents" | Consumer advocates estimate the overbuild could raise household bills **~$3.4B / ~$20 per home-month** (SACE–Sierra Club / JD Supra briefs, Dec 2025). |
| "Data centers are fully responsible for their own utility costs" | True on the *rate* side — but Microsoft already got **$75M / 10-yr** tax abatement, and the PSC interconnection cost-allocation fight (#56008, #56298/#56310) is literally about whether *grid* costs get socialized to homes. |

## 5. HOW THIS FEEDS THE CAMPAIGN (new asks)

1. **Water-resilience conditions on any DC approval** — cooling-water limits, on-site backup power, and an independent infrastructure-impact study *before* rezoning.
2. **No new abatements until the water/power system is hardened** — the "you pay nothing extra" promise is already strained.
3. **EPD + regional water authority pressure** (leverage #6 in the case study) — demand DWM/EPD release water-system reliability data alongside any DC capacity request.
4. **Use the boil advisories as organizing proof** — a real, visceral, nonpartisan issue every Union City resident just lived through. It converts "data center" from abstract to personal.
