# LETTER PACK — Union City Data Center Fight

---

## LETTER 1 — Union City Mayor & Council (PRIMARY)

Dear Mayor Williams and Members of Council,

First — thank you. The unanimous 5-0 vote on March 24 to adopt the 180-day moratorium was the right call, and it's the kind of leadership this moment requires. I'm a Union City resident writing to ask you to finish the job.

That moratorium is expiring now. Before it lapses, I'm asking for three things:

1. EXTEND the moratorium until the zoning rewrite is complete and adopted. Don't reopen the door mid-process.
2. In the rewrite, classify data centers as a heavy-industrial / conditional use — not by-right. That means special-use permits, public hearings, and hard conditions: setbacks, noise limits, water and cooling restrictions, and screening. Not a rubber stamp.
3. Draw a hard line on the Buffington Road / Jonesboro Road proposal. The community has already shown up against it. Please don't let it through on the Zoning Commission's recommendation alone.

The pattern across the country is clear: cities that paused and reclassified won — Denver, Eagan MN, Ypsilanti MI, Coweta County's Project Oak denial. Cities that didn't are now living with 24/7 noise, drained water systems, and higher power bills.

Union City doesn't have to be an industrial sacrifice zone for companies that collect $75 million in abatements and give nothing back in quality of life. We can zone them out.

I'll be at the next council meeting, and I'm organizing neighbors to be there too. Tell me what you need from us to make the permanent protections happen.

## LETTER 2 — Fulton County Commission

Dear Chairman Pitts, Commissioner Arrington, and Commissioner Abdur-Rahman,

I'm writing as a Union City resident about the data center buildout consuming south Fulton. There are roughly twenty proposed data centers in Fulton County, and the Board's own Development Authority has already handed Microsoft $75 million in tax savings for a single Union City campus.

I'm asking for three concrete actions:

1. A countywide moratorium on new data center approvals, matching what Union City did locally.
2. A freeze on future data center tax abatements through the Development Authority of Fulton County. These abatements are subsidizing multinational corporations out of our tax base.
3. Real scrutiny at the DRI level for every proposed data center, with public hearings held in the affected communities — not downtown.

South Fulton already carries the county's warehouses and heavy industry. Stacking twenty data centers on top — with their water draw and 24/7 diesel backups — is an environmental justice issue for majority-Black communities that have heard "no" far less often than the northside.

I'll be following the Development Authority's abatement votes closely.

## LETTER 3 — State Senator Rashaun Kemp (D-38) — the champion ask

Dear Senator Kemp,

You represent the exact corridor this fight is about — Union City, Fairburn, East Point, South Fulton, Palmetto. And you've already proven you can pass bills in your first session. I'm asking you to make data centers your signature issue in 2027.

The state moratorium bills died this session (HB 1012, HB 1059), which pushed the fight onto local governments where it shouldn't have to live alone. Georgia Power is planning 10,000 megawatts of new capacity — 80% of it for data centers — and the bill for that infrastructure is drifting toward residential ratepayers, not the companies demanding it.

I'm asking you to refile and champion a bill that does four things:

1. A statewide moratorium on new data center permits (renewing what Rep. Romman and Rep. Jackson proposed).
2. Explicit local control — no state preemption of municipal zoning authority on data centers.
3. Ratepayer protection — data centers pay the full cost of their interconnection and transmission, not a dime socialized onto home bills.
4. A sunset of the sales-tax break subsidizing them (the HB 559 ask).

This is a winnable, populist issue that crosses party lines — rising power bills and industrial sprawl don't care about the letter next to a name. My community will organize behind whoever leads it. I want that to be you.

I'd like to set a meeting to walk through what's already been filed and what needs strengthening. Your legislative assistant Cole Simmons can reach me directly.

## LETTER 5 — Georgia Public Service Commission (all five commissioners)

Dear Chairman Shaw, Vice Chairman McDonald, Commissioners Hubbard, Johnson, and Wise,

I'm a Georgia Power customer in Union City, writing on the dockets that will decide whether my neighbors and I pay for the data center buildout: the 2025 IRP (#56002), the 2029–2031 capacity certifications (#56298, #56310), and the large-load rules (#56008).

The numbers are public record: Georgia Power is planning 10,000 MW of new capacity, roughly 80% for data centers, at a cost exceeding $15 billion. The question isn't whether data centers come to Georgia. The question is who pays.

I'm asking the Commission to hold the line on three principles:

1. Data centers pay the full cost of their interconnection and transmission infrastructure. No cost-shifting to residential and small-business ratepayers.
2. The proposed 3.2-gigawatt OpenAI contract gets the same public scrutiny as any IRP resource — not approved behind a nondisclosure agreement. Commissioner Hubbard raised this exact concern, and he's right.
3. Approve no additional fossil fuel capacity (the #56298 / #56310 certifications) until the cost-allocation question is settled.

Overbuilding to chase demand that may not materialize could raise household bills by billions — roughly $20 a month per home by some estimates — while the companies that caused it shelter behind abatements. That's not a fair grid. That's a subsidy.

I'll be filing formal comments in these dockets and asking my community to do the same.

## LETTER 6 — Governor Brian Kemp

Dear Governor Kemp,

I'm writing as a Union City resident about the fastest-growing strain on Georgia's grid and Georgia's communities: hyperscale data centers.

The state's own numbers tell the story — prospective large-load projects grew from 16,000 MW in 2023 to 69,000 MW by December 2025, roughly 80% of it data centers. Georgia Power says it needs 10,000 MW of new capacity and $15 billion to serve it. And this session, the legislature's moratorium and impact-assessment bills all died, pushing the entire burden onto local governments.

In Union City, our council was forced to pass its own emergency moratorium because the state offers no backstop. That isn't sustainable, and it isn't fair to leave every small city to fight the largest companies on earth alone.

I'm asking you to take two actions:

1. Direct an emergency statewide review of data center impacts on grid reliability, water, and residential rates.
2. Support a statewide moratorium and siting standards in the 2027 session so municipalities aren't fighting this alone.

Georgia can welcome data centers without sacrificing its residents. Right now it's doing the opposite.

## LETTER 8 — Union City Mayor & Council (water resilience)

Dear Mayor Williams and Members of Council,

On October 11, our city went under its third boil-water advisory of the year — this one from a water main break at the Chattahoochee Water Treatment Plant, following May and July advisories caused by power failures at Atlanta water facilities. Three times in five months, Union City residents were told their water might not be safe. Each time the immediate cause was aging infrastructure, not any single project.

But here's why I'm writing to you about it in the context of data centers: our water and power systems just showed us they are already near their edge. We are being asked to approve industrial-scale data centers — with their enormous 24/7 power draw and their millions of gallons a day in cooling water — stacked on top of a system that has now failed three times in one year.

The city's own "Understanding Data Centers" page promises residents "no increase in electric or water bills" and "no environmental risks found." I ask you to stand behind that promise with conditions, not with a brochure.

Before any further data center is approved, I'm asking Council to require:

1. An independent water and grid impact study — paid for by the applicant, reviewed by EPD and our regional water authority — before any rezoning is heard.
2. Hard cooling-water limits and guaranteed on-site backup power as conditions of approval, not aspirations.
3. A pause on new tax abatements until the water and power system is demonstrably hardened. A $75 million abatement is a subsidy; a water system that can't hold pressure is a liability.

The boil advisory isn't the data centers' fault — not yet. It's the warning. I don't want Union City to be the city that ignored the warning and then had to explain to families why the water stopped and the bills went up at the same time.

I'll be at the next council meeting, and I'll bring neighbors who lived through the same three advisories.

— Jalen Lee

1. **Verify your exact districts.** Enter your address at house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative — US House = GA-13 (Rep. Everton Blair); State House = D-67 (Rep. Glaize); State Senate = D-38 (Sen. Kemp).
2. **Address + phone already filled** — personalize the first line of each instead.
3. **Personalize the first line** — a one-sentence specific detail about your block/street beats a template.
4. **Send each as a separate, distinct message** — identical mass letters get logged and ignored. These already have distinct asks.
5. **Follow up.** Call the district offices 3–5 days after sending. A call gets logged; a letter alone can sit.
6. **Show up.** Union City council meets the 3rd Tuesday, 6pm work session / 7pm regular. Fulton County Commission: 1st & 3rd Wednesday, 10am. Pack both.
