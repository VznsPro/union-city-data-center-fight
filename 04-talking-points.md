# TALKING POINTS — In-Person (Union City + Fulton + State)

Print this. Bring it with the documents. This is what you say when you get in front of someone.

---

## 30-second version (the elevator)

I'm a Union City resident here about the data-center buildout — and about our water.

Three things are true right now:
1. Georgia Power is planning 10,000 megawatts of new power, 80% of it for data centers — over $15 billion.
2. Union City already has a $1.8 billion Microsoft campus with a $75 million tax break, and 20 more projects are queued behind it.
3. Our city just went under its **third boil-water advisory of the year** — and the moratorium protecting us is expiring.

I'm asking you to extend the moratorium, and require water and power safeguards before any data center is approved.

---

## The three facts (know them cold)

- **10,000 MW / $15B / 80%.** Georgia Power's own planning. 80% of new demand is data centers.
- **$75M abatement.** Microsoft's Union City campus already got a 10-year tax break. The companies profiting are subsidized; the residents carry the grid and water risk.
- **3 boil advisories in 5 months.** May (Hemphill WTP power failure), July (Adamsville pump power failure), October (Chattahoochee main break). The system is already at its edge.

## The three asks (match them to the room)

| Room | Ask |
|---|---|
| Union City Council | Extend the moratorium → make data centers a conditional/heavy-industrial use with water + power + noise conditions |
| Fulton Commission | Countywide moratorium + freeze Development Authority abatements |
| State (Kemp/Glaize/Romman/Jackson) | Refile the moratorium + local control + ratepayer protection in 2027 |
| PSC | Data centers pay full interconnection/transmission cost — no cost-shift to home bills |

## The water line (say it EXACTLY like this — it's honest and it lands)

> The boil advisory isn't the data centers' fault — *yet.* It's the canary. Our water and power already failed three times this year. We should not stack industrial-scale demand on top of a system that's already at its edge.

**Why this wording:** it never claims the data center caused the boil advisory (that's false and they'll catch it). It converts the advisory into proof of fragility, which is the real argument.

## If they push back (the city's own talking points, answered)

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "No environmental risks have been found." | "Not found by whom, and under what standard? Three boil advisories in five months is a risk we just lived through. 'No risk found' isn't the same as 'no risk.'" |
| "There will be no increase in your bills." | "Consumer advocates put the overbuild at ~$3.4 billion — roughly $20 a month per home. The cost is real; the question is who pays." |
| "Data centers pay their own utility costs." | "On the rate, yes. But the $75 million abatement is a subsidy, and the PSC is literally fighting over whether grid costs get socialized to homes. It's not settled." |
| "This is about jobs and progress." | "Progress doesn't require making our water unreliable. We can welcome data centers with conditions — setbacks, water limits, backup power — instead of a rubber stamp." |
| "The state preempts you." | "That's exactly the problem. The 2026 moratorium bills died, so it's on us locally. We're asking you to back us up, not leave us alone." |
